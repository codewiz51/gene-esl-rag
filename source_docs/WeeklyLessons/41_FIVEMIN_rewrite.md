# MONDAY

## Vocabulary Review

1. a little off – no muy bien / un poco rara
2. let's get the ball rolling – vamos a empezar / manos a la obra
3. light-headed – mareado / mareada
4. warning sign – señal de alerta

## Grammar Focus

Marisol feels a little off, and Mrs. Carter feels light-headed. Each woman is "she," so the verb is "feels."

## Mini Story

1. Marisol feels a little off because she did not sleep well.
2. She says "Let's get the ball rolling," a phrase from Rosie.
3. Mrs. Carter tells Marisol that she feels light-headed today.
4. Marisol takes her pulse, and it is 112 beats per minute.
5. A fast pulse is a warning sign, and she tells Letia.
6. Marisol writes a short note in the chart for Dr. Pérez.

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. Marisol no se siente muy bien porque no durmió bien.
2. La señora Carter se siente mareada y tiene el pulso rápido.

English → Spanish

1. The normal range for a pulse is 60 to 100.

## Student Questions

1. What is Mrs. Carter's pulse? (latidos)
2. How do you feel today? (sentirse)

# TUESDAY

## Vocabulary Review

1. stomach cramps – retortijones / cólicos
2. weak – débil
3. pale – pálido / pálida
4. check in – registrar al paciente

## Grammar Focus

Mr. Delgado tells Marisol about his stomach cramps. He is one person, so the verb is "tells."

## Mini Story

1. Marisol arrives with her coffee after a long night.
2. Mr. Delgado looks pale, and he holds his stomach.
3. He tells Marisol about his stomach cramps and says he feels weak.
4. Marisol checks him in and takes his blood pressure.
5. His blood pressure is 98 over 60, and it is low.
6. She tells Letia and writes the numbers in the chart.

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. El señor Delgado está pálido y se agarra la barriga.
2. Marisol le dice a Letia que la presión está baja.

English → Spanish

1. Marisol says, "No worries. I will check you in."

## Student Questions

1. What does Mr. Delgado tell Marisol? (barriga)
2. Who do you tell when you feel weak? (débil)
