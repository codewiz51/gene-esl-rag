# MONDAY

## Warmup

1. I ___ his blood pressure every morning. (tomo)
2. When the waiting room is full, I say, "Please ___ a moment." (espere)
3. I ___ the patient's temperature with the thermometer. (tomo)

## Vocabulary

1. blood pressure cuff – manguito de presión
2. thermometer – termómetro
3. stethoscope – estetoscopio
4. take a seat – tomar asiento
5. please wait a moment / hang on a sec – espere un momento / espera un segundo (formal / casual)

## Story

**English:**

Monday is carpool day. Marisol drives Camy and Donna to school. The girls laugh in the back seat the whole way.

At the clinic, the waiting room is full before 9 a.m. An elderly man stands near the door with his cane. There are no empty chairs. Marisol walks over and says, "Please wait a moment." She finds him a chair by the window. Rosie leans over and whispers, "Hang on a sec, I'll grab his chart."

Soon the man is in the exam room. He asks if he can take a seat closer to the door. Marisol says yes and moves the chair for him. She takes his blood pressure with the cuff. The cuff makes a soft hiss. Next, she takes his temperature with the thermometer. Then she listens to his lungs with the stethoscope.

Through the door, Marisol sees the full waiting room. It is going to be a long Monday, she thinks. She writes the numbers on the iPad before Dra. Pérez comes in.

**Spanish:**

El lunes le toca a Marisol llevar a las niñas. Lleva a Camy y a Donna a la escuela. Las niñas se ríen en el asiento de atrás todo el camino.

En la clínica, la sala de espera está llena antes de las 9 de la mañana. Un señor mayor está parado cerca de la puerta con su bastón. No hay sillas vacías. Marisol se acerca y le dice: "Espere un momento, por favor." Le busca una silla al lado de la ventana. Rosie se inclina y le susurra: "Espera un segundo, voy a buscar su expediente."

Al rato, el señor ya está en la sala de examen. Pregunta si puede tomar asiento más cerca de la puerta. Marisol le dice que sí y le mueve la silla. Le toma la presión con el manguito. El manguito hace un silbidito suave. Después le toma la temperatura con el termómetro. Luego le escucha los pulmones con el estetoscopio.

Por la puerta, Marisol ve la sala de espera llena. Va a ser un lunes largo, piensa. Anota los números en el iPad antes de que entre la Dra. Pérez.

## Grammar

"Take" tiene dos significados: tomar y llevar; esta semana nos concentramos en tomar, para los signos vitales. Con he, she o it, añadimos una -s: I take, she takes. Por ejemplo: "Marisol takes his blood pressure."

Pattern Practice:

1. Marisol ___ his blood pressure with the cuff. (toma)
2. Next, I ___ his temperature. (tomaré)
3. The patient ___ a seat closer to the door. (toma)

## Examples

1. I take his blood pressure every morning.

2. She took Camy to school today.

3. I will take his temperature next.

4. Marisol takes the patient's blood pressure with the cuff.

5. Marisol and Rosie talk in the waiting room.
Marisol: "Please wait a moment, sir. We have no chairs right now."
Rosie: "Hang on a sec, I'll grab his chart."
Marisol: "Thanks. I'll take his blood pressure first."
Rosie: "Ugh, the waiting room is full, and it's not even nine."
Marisol: "I know. It's going to be a long Monday."

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. El lunes le toca a Marisol llevar a las niñas.
2. Un señor mayor está parado cerca de la puerta con su bastón.
3. Marisol se acerca y le dice: "Espere un momento, por favor."
4. Pregunta si puede tomar asiento más cerca de la puerta.
5. Le toma la presión con el manguito.
6. Anota los números en el iPad antes de que entre la Dra. Pérez.

English → Spanish

1. Next, she takes his temperature with the thermometer.
2. The waiting room is full before 9 a.m.

## Student Questions

1. Did you help someone take a seat today? (tomar asiento)
2. What do you say when a patient needs to wait? (espere un momento)
3. Can you use the stethoscope to listen to a patient's lungs? (estetoscopio)
4. Do you use "please wait a moment" with patients and "hang on a sec" with coworkers? (registro)
5. Did you take a patient's blood pressure with the cuff today? (manguito)

# TUESDAY

## Warmup

1. I ___ the grocery list before I go to the store. (reviso)
2. I ___ the electric bill, and I sigh. (reviso)

## Vocabulary

1. results – resultados
2. check the results / look into it – revisar los resultados (formal / casual)
3. grocery list – lista de compras
4. electric bill – cuenta de electricidad

## Story

**English:**

After work, Marisol checks the grocery list before she goes to the store. At the store, she checks the price of chicken and eggs. Both went up again. At home, she checks the electric bill and sighs. Then Rosie texts her and asks if the clinic got the patient's results back. Marisol types, "I'll check the results tomorrow morning." Rosie replies, "You could just say 'I'll look into it,' like on TV." Marisol laughs and puts her phone down.

**Spanish:**

Después del trabajo, Marisol revisa la lista de compras antes de ir a la tienda. En la tienda, revisa el precio del pollo y de los huevos. Los dos subieron otra vez. En la casa, revisa la cuenta de electricidad y suspira. Entonces Rosie le escribe y le pregunta si a la clínica ya le llegaron los resultados del paciente. Marisol escribe: "Voy a revisar los resultados mañana por la mañana." Rosie le contesta: "Puedes decir nada más 'I'll look into it,' como en la tele." Marisol se ríe y deja el teléfono.

## Grammar

"Check" significa revisar. Con he o she, añadimos una -s: I check, she checks. "I'll check the results" es la forma formal, y "I'll look into it" es la forma casual.

Pattern Practice:

1. Marisol ___ the price of chicken and eggs. (revisa)
2. Tomorrow morning, I ___ the results. (revisaré)

## Examples

1. I check the results before I call the patient.

2. Marisol checks her budget every Tuesday night.

3. Rosie and Marisol text after work.
Rosie: "Hey, did the clinic get the patient's results back?"
Marisol: "I'll check the results tomorrow morning."
Rosie: "Well, you could just say 'I'll look into it,' like on TV."
Marisol: "Ha! Okay. I'll look into it."
Rosie: "See? Much easier."

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. En la tienda, revisa el precio del pollo y de los huevos.
2. Entonces Rosie le escribe y le pregunta si a la clínica ya le llegaron los resultados del paciente.

English → Spanish

1. At home, she checks the electric bill and sighs.
2. I'll check the results tomorrow morning.

## Student Questions

1. Did you check the grocery list before you went to the store today? (lista de compras)
2. What do you say when you need to check the results? (revisar)
3. Is your electric bill higher this month? (cuenta de electricidad)
4. Do you prefer "check the results" or "look into it"? (preferencia)

# THURSDAY

## Warmup

1. I ___ the instructions to the patient before he leaves. (explico)
2. The doctor asks me to ___ his vitals again. (tomar)
3. I ___ the pulse oximeter reading. (reviso)

## Vocabulary

1. pulse oximeter – oxímetro de pulso
2. otoscope – otoscopio
3. recovering / bouncing back – recuperándose (formal / casual)
4. monitor the patient / keep an eye on them – vigilar al paciente / estar pendiente de él (formal / casual)

## Story

**English:**

Marisol checks the iPad for today's patients. One is a follow-up for a broken arm. Dra. Pérez asks Marisol to take his vitals and check the pulse oximeter reading. Marisol puts the small clip on the man's finger. It beeps and shows 98 percent. That is a good number.

The man looks at Dra. Pérez and asks, "How am I doing, Doctora?" She smiles and says, "You're recovering well." Later, in the hallway, she tells Rosie, "He's bouncing back fast."

Back in the room, Marisol explains, "The doctor wants us to monitor your arm for two more weeks." The man looks confused. Amanda is at the door. She whispers to Marisol, "Just keep an eye on the swelling." Marisol tries again: "We will keep an eye on the swelling." Now the man nods.

Before he leaves, Marisol checks his ear with the otoscope, as Dra. Pérez asked. Everything looks fine. Marisol feels proud. She explained it in English, two ways.

**Spanish:**

Marisol revisa en el iPad los pacientes del día. Uno viene a una consulta de seguimiento por un brazo roto. La Dra. Pérez le pide a Marisol que le tome los signos vitales y que revise la lectura del oxímetro de pulso. Marisol le pone la pinza chiquita en el dedo. Suena un bip y marca 98 por ciento. Ese número está bien.

El señor mira a la Dra. Pérez y le pregunta: "¿Cómo voy, doctora?" Ella sonríe y le dice: "Se está recuperando bien." Más tarde, en el pasillo, le dice a Rosie: "Se está recuperando rapidísimo."

De vuelta en la sala, Marisol le explica: "La doctora quiere que vigilemos su brazo dos semanas más." El señor se queda confundido. Amanda está en la puerta. Le susurra a Marisol: "Just keep an eye on the swelling." Marisol lo intenta otra vez: "We will keep an eye on the swelling." Ahora el señor asiente.

Antes de que se vaya, Marisol le revisa el oído con el otoscopio, como le pidió la Dra. Pérez. Todo se ve bien. Marisol se siente orgullosa. Lo explicó en inglés, de dos maneras.

## Grammar

"Explain" significa explicar. Con he o she, añadimos una -s: I explain, she explains. Ojo: decimos "explain it to him," nunca "explain him."

Pattern Practice:

1. Marisol ___ the plan to the patient. (explica)
2. She will ___ the results after the doctor checks them. (explicar)
3. Dra. Pérez asks her to ___ his vitals again. (tomar)

## Examples

1. Marisol explains the instructions to the patient.

2. She will explain the results after the doctor checks them.

3. The doctor asked her to take his vitals again.

4. Marisol and Amanda talk at the door.
Marisol: "I said 'monitor your arm,' and he looked confused."
Amanda: "Just say 'keep an eye on the swelling.' It's easier."
Marisol: "Keep an eye on the swelling. Okay."
Amanda: "See? Now he understands. You explained it two ways."
Marisol: "Ha! Two ways for one sentence. English!"

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. Uno viene a una consulta de seguimiento por un brazo roto.
2. La Dra. Pérez le pide a Marisol que le tome los signos vitales y que revise la lectura del oxímetro de pulso.
3. Marisol le pone la pinza chiquita en el dedo.
4. Más tarde, en el pasillo, le dice a Rosie: "Se está recuperando rapidísimo."
5. El señor se queda confundido.
6. Antes de que se vaya, Marisol le revisa el oído con el otoscopio, como le pidió la Dra. Pérez.

English → Spanish

1. The doctor wants us to monitor your arm for two more weeks.
2. She smiles and says, "You're recovering well."

## Student Questions

# FRIDAY

## Warmup

1. I ___ the co-pay to the patient at the front desk. (explico)
2. I ___ the schedule before I start work. (reviso)
3. I ___ the patient's blood pressure twice to be sure. (tomo)

## Vocabulary

1. front desk – recepción
2. co-pay – copago
3. the doctor is unavailable / the doctor is tied up – la doctora no está disponible / la doctora está ocupada (formal / casual)
4. confused – confundido

## Story

**English:**

It is 3:40 on a busy Friday afternoon. The phone rings. A patient's husband asks to speak to Dra. Pérez directly. Marisol checks the whiteboard. Dra. Pérez is with a patient. Marisol says, "The doctor is unavailable right now. I'll have the doctor call you back."

Rosie mutters, "Just tell him she's tied up." Amanda adds, "We should pull the HIPAA form for the doctor. She'll need to see it before she talks to the husband."

At the front desk, a man looks at his bill. He is confused about his co-pay. Marisol explains it to him slowly. She points to each number on the paper. Then she takes his insurance card and copies it for the file. The man says, "Okay. Now I understand."

At 5:00, the clinic is finally quiet. Marisol counts on her fingers. Today she took vitals for eleven patients. Her feet hurt, but she feels good.

**Spanish:**

Son las 3:40 de un viernes por la tarde con mucho trabajo. Suena el teléfono. El esposo de una paciente pide hablar directamente con la Dra. Pérez. Marisol revisa la pizarra. La Dra. Pérez está con un paciente. Marisol le dice: "La doctora no está disponible ahora mismo. Le voy a decir que lo llame."

Rosie murmura: "Just tell him she's tied up." Amanda agrega: "Hay que sacar la planilla de HIPAA pa' la doctora. Ella tiene que verla antes de hablar con el esposo."

En la recepción, un señor mira su cuenta. Está confundido con el copago. Marisol se lo explica despacito. Le señala cada número en el papel. Después le toma la tarjeta del seguro y le saca una copia pa'l expediente. El señor dice: "Ah, ya entiendo."

A las 5:00, por fin la clínica está tranquila. Marisol cuenta con los dedos. Hoy les tomó los signos vitales a once pacientes. Le duelen los pies, pero se siente bien.

## Grammar

Hoy usamos take, check y explain en presente, pasado y futuro. En pasado, "take" es irregular: took. "Check" y "explain" llevan -ed: checked, explained.

Pattern Practice:

1. Marisol ___ the whiteboard. (revisa)
2. Yesterday, Marisol ___ the co-pay to a confused patient. (explicó)
3. Today, she ___ vitals for eleven patients. (tomó)

## Examples

1. Marisol took the patient's blood pressure twice to be sure.

2. She will take the new patient's information at the front desk.

3. Rosie checked the schedule and found a mistake.

4. Marisol explained the co-pay to a confused patient.

5. Marisol, Rosie, and Amanda talk at the front desk.
Marisol: "I told him, 'The doctor is unavailable right now.'"
Rosie: "Just tell him she's tied up."
Marisol: "That's the casual way, right?"
Rosie: "Yep. Same thing, fewer words."
Amanda: "Well, first we should pull the HIPAA form for the doctor."

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. El esposo de una paciente pide hablar directamente con la Dra. Pérez.
2. Marisol revisa la pizarra.
3. Amanda agrega: "Hay que sacar la planilla de HIPAA pa' la doctora."
4. Está confundido con el copago.
5. Después le toma la tarjeta del seguro y le saca una copia pa'l expediente.
6. Hoy les tomó los signos vitales a once pacientes.
