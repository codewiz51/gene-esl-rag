# MONDAY

## Warmup

1. Something ___ when you are not careful. (pasa)
2. I am ___ to school when an accident happens. (tardes)
3. I see ___ when a child gets a scraped knee. (lágrimas)

## Vocabulary

1. scraped knee – rodilla raspada
2. non-stick gauze pad – gasa que no se pega
3. gauze tape – cinta para gasa
4. tears – lágrimas
5. late to school – tarde a la escuela

## Story

**English:**

Monday is carpool day. Marisa's van waits at the end of the street. Camy and Donna run outside before the alarm finishes. Camy stops on the sidewalk to show Donna a cartwheel from PE class. She kicks one leg up, then the other, and for a second she is upside down. Then her foot hits a landscape stone and she falls hard.

Marisol hears the crying from the kitchen and runs outside. She finds Camy on the concrete, tears on her face, jeans torn at the knee. The scraped knee is red and raw. Marisol cleans it with warm water, puts a non-stick gauze pad over it, and wraps it with gauze tape. Camy keeps crying, and the tears soak through the first pad, so Marisol changes it.

By the time they get in the car, they are late to school. Camy looks at her mother.

"I'm sorry, Mama. I just wanted to show Donna."

Marisol puts her hand on Camy's head. Accidents happen. She turns the key and drives them to school, two minutes past the bell.

**Spanish:**

Lunes es día de compartir carro pa' la escuela. La van de Marisa espera al final de la calle. Camy y Donna corren pa' afuera antes de que suene la alarma. Camy se detiene en la acera pa' mostrarle a Donna una voltereta que aprendió en educación física. Levanta una pierna, luego la otra, y un segundo está boca abajo. Entonces su pie choca con una piedra del jardín y se cae fuerte.

Marisol oye el llanto desde la cocina y sale corriendo. Encuentra a Camy en el concreto, con lágrimas en la cara, los jeans rotos en la rodilla. La rodilla raspada está roja y abierta. Marisol la limpia con agua tibia, le pone una gasa que no se pega encima y la envuelve con cinta para gasa. Camy sigue llorando, y las lágrimas mojan la primera gasa, así que Marisol la cambia.

Pa' cuando suben al carro, están tardes pa' la escuela. Camy mira a su mamá.

"Perdóname, Mama. Solo quería mostrarle a Donna."

Marisol le pone la mano en la cabeza a Camy. Los accidentes pasan. Da la llave y las lleva a las dos a la escuela, dos minutos después del timbre.

## Grammar

El verbo "happen" quiere decir "pasar" en español. Es un verbo regular, así que el pasado es "happened". Se usa pa' hablar de cosas que pasan, sobre todo accidentes o sorpresas.

Pattern Practice:

1. Something ___ to Camy before school. (pasó)
2. What ___ to your knee this morning? (pasó)
3. Accidents ___ at the clinic every day. (pasan)

## Examples

1. Camy does a cartwheel on the sidewalk and falls on a stone.
2. Marisol cleans the wound with warm water and wraps it with tape.
3. They drive to school two minutes after the bell.
4. Marisol and Camy talk after the accident.
Marisol: "Camy, look at your knee. It is bleeding."
Camy: "I know, Mama. I just wanted to show Donna the cartwheel."
Marisol: "I know, mija. Accidents happen. Let me clean it."
Camy: "It hurts when you put the water on it."
Marisol: "I know. Hold still, and it will be okay."

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. La paciente necesita que le limpien la herida con agua tibia antes de ponerle la venda.
2. Camy se cae al hacer una voltereta en la acera.
3. La mamá le pone una gasa encima de la rodilla roja.
4. Ellas llegan a la escuela dos minutos después del timbre.
5. La niña llora porque le duele mucho la rodilla.
6. La mamá le dice: "Los accidentes pasan, mija."

English → Spanish

1. She cleans the wound with warm water and puts a pad over it.
2. The girls run outside before the alarm finishes.

## Student Questions

1. Did your child get a scraped knee this week? (rodilla raspada)
2. What do you do when your child has tears on her face after a fall? (lágrimas)
3. Did you arrive late to school when you were a child? (tarde a la escuela)
4. What do you use to hold a non-stick gauze pad on a wound? (cinta para gasa)

# TUESDAY

## Warmup

1. My knee ___ after I fell yesterday. (lastimó)
2. I cannot ___ in PE when my knee hurts. (participar)

## Vocabulary

1. strawberry – raspón, no "fresa" (una abrasión sangrante con pérdida superficial de la piel)
2. participate – participar
3. heal – sanar
4. cheesy – cursi

## Story

**English:**

The next morning, Marisol checks Camy's knee. The strawberry is smaller and a scab is forming, so she puts petroleum jelly on it and a fresh bandage. Camy says it still hurts a little, but she can walk fine. She goes to school with the injured knee and tells her teacher she will not participate in PE today.

At the clinic, Rosie turns up the radio, and a pop song comes on. "This is so cheesy," Rosie says. Marisol does not know what "cheesy" means, so she asks Rosie to explain. Rosie laughs and says the music is too sweet, too much. Marisol nods, not sure she gets it.

**Spanish:**

La mañana siguiente, Marisol revisa la rodilla de Camy. El raspón está más pequeño y ya se está formando una costra, así que le pone vaselina y una venda nueva. Camy dice que todavía le duele un poquito, pero puede caminar bien. Va a la escuela con la rodilla lastimada y le dice a su maestra que no va a participar en educación física hoy.

En la clínica, Rosie sube el volumen de la radio y sale una canción pop. "Esto es muy cursi," dice Rosie. Marisol no sabe qué significa cursi, así que le pide a Rosie que se lo explique. Rosie se ríe y le dice que la música es muy dulce, de más. Marisol asiente, sin estar segura de entenderlo.

## Grammar

El verbo "hurt" quiere decir "doler" o "lastimar" en español. Es un verbo irregular: hurt, hurt, hurt — el pasado se ve igual que el presente. También se puede usar "hurt" como adjetivo, como en "My knee is hurt".

Pattern Practice:

1. Camy ___ her knee yesterday. (lastimó)
2. I ___ my back when I lift heavy boxes. (duele)
3. Something ___ at the clinic this morning. (pasó)

## Examples

1. Camy can walk fine, but the knee still hurts a little.
2. She tells her teacher she will not participate in PE today.
3. Marisol and Rosie talk at the clinic.
Rosie: "Ugh, this song is so cheesy, Marisol."
Marisol: "Cheesy? What does that even mean?"
Rosie: "You know, like... too sweet. Too much. It's trying too hard."
Marisol: "I just hear a pop song. I don't get it."
Rosie: "Stop thinking about it, mija. Just let it hit you."

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. La maestra le dice a Camy que no va a participar en educación física hoy porque le duele la rodilla.
2. La mamá le pone vaselina y una venda nueva a la rodilla de Camy.

English → Spanish

1. The knee is getting better, but it still hurts when she bends it.
2. Rosie turns up the radio and a pop song comes on.

## Student Questions

1. Does your child ever say a song is cheesy? (cursi)
2. What do you tell your child when she cannot participate in PE because of a hurt knee? (participar)
3. How long does a scraped knee take to heal in your experience? (sanar)

# WEDNESDAY

## Warmup

1. My knee still ___ a little this morning. (duele)
2. I ___ the wound before I put a bandage on it. (atender)

## Vocabulary

1. take a Tylenol – tomarse una pastilla de Tylenol
2. attend to the wound – atender la herida
3. petroleum jelly – vaselina
4. cheesy – cursi

## Story

**English:**

Camy's knee is healing, but it still hurts when she bends it. Marisol gives her a Tylenol and attends to the wound with petroleum jelly and a new bandage. She thinks about how the accident happened, the cartwheel, the stone, the fall. She is thankful that no one was seriously hurt. Donna was not hurt at all.

At the clinic, the radio plays the same pop song from Tuesday. Marisol still does not understand why Rosie called it cheesy. She asks Letia, and Letia shrugs and says, "You just have to feel it." Marisol drinks her coffee and decides to let it go.

**Spanish:**

La rodilla de Camy se está sanando, pero todavía le duele cuando la dobla. Marisol le da una pastilla de Tylenol y atiende la herida con vaselina y una venda nueva. Piensa en cómo pasó el accidente, la voltereta, la piedra, la caída. Está agradecida de que a nadie le pasó algo serio. A Donna no le pasó nada.

En la clínica, la radio suena la misma canción pop de martes. Marisol todavía no entiende por qué Rosie la llamó cursi. Le pregunta a Letia, y Letia encoge los hombros y dice: "Hay que sentirlo." Marisol toma su café y decide dejarlo estar.

## Grammar

Hoy repasamos "happen" (pasar) y "hurt" (doler / lastimar). Los dos verbos se usan mucho en historias de accidentes. "Happen" es regular, así que el pasado es "happened"; "hurt" es irregular, así que el pasado sigue siendo "hurt".

Pattern Practice:

1. Camy's accident ___ before school. (pasó)
2. If you scrape your knee, it can ___. (doler)
3. The nurse ___ the wound with petroleum jelly. (atender)

## Examples

1. Camy's knee is healing, but it still hurts when she bends it.
2. Marisol decides to let the song go and drinks her coffee.
3. Marisol and Letia talk at the clinic.

Letia: "I don't know, I just feel it when I hear it."
Marisol: "I don't feel it. I just hear a pop song."
Letia: "Well, you should stop thinking and just listen."
Marisol: "Okay, I will try. But I still don't get it."

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. La enfermera le da una pastilla de Tylenol a la paciente y le pone vaselina en la herida.
2. La canción suena otra vez en la radio y Marisol no entiende por qué es cursi.

English → Spanish

1. The nurse cleans the wound and puts a new bandage on it.
2. Marisol drinks her coffee and decides to let it go.

## Student Questions

1. Do you take a Tylenol when you have a headache at work? (pastilla de Tylenol)
2. What do you put on a small wound to help it heal? (vaselina)

# THURSDAY

## Warmup

1. I ___ the kitchen table every morning. (limpio)
2. My knee is ___ after the accident. (mejorando)
3. I ___ when the doctor explains the medicine. (entender)

## Vocabulary

1. get better – mejorarse
2. over-the-counter – sin receta
3. get it – entender
4. prescription – receta

## Story

**English:**

Marisol walks Camy to Marisa's apartment. It is Marisa's turn to take the girls to school. Camy's knee is getting better, and she hops only a little on the sidewalk. Marisol goes to the clinic, and there are already eight cars in the lot.

Most of the patients will have blood drawn, and several are there for DOT checkups. Mr. Reyes comes in for sciatica. The pain is bad, and he wants something strong. Dra. Pérez tells him the clinic can only authorize over-the-counter medicine, 800 mg of ibuprofen, not a prescription painkiller.

"Just ibuprofen? That's it?" Mr. Reyes says, and he does not look happy.

Marisol explains the dosage. The printer buzzes. She hands him the printout and tells him: two tablets with food, three times a day. He reads it, frowns, and says, "I don't get it." Marisol smiles. "You will get it. Try it for a week." He leaves grumbling, but he takes the printout.

The afternoon is busy with school vaccinations and runny noses. Marisol cleans the exam rooms between patients and doesn't sit until four.

**Spanish:**

Marisol lleva a Camy al apartamento de Marisa. Es turno de Marisa pa' llevar a las niñas a la escuela. La rodilla de Camy se está mejorando, y solo cojea un poquito en la acera. Marisol va directo a la clínica, y ya hay ocho carros en el estacionamiento.

La mayoría de los pacientes van a sacarse sangre, y varios están pa' los chequeos del DOT. El señor Reyes llega por la ciática. El dolor es fuerte, y quiere algo más fuerte. La Dra. Pérez le dice que la clínica solo puede autorizar medicina sin receta, 800 mg de ibuprofeno, no un analgésico con receta.

"¿Solo ibuprofeno? ¿Eso es todo?" dice el señor Reyes, y no se ve contento.

Marisol le explica la dosis. La impresora zumba. Le entrega el impreso y le dice: dos pastillas con comida, tres veces al día. Él lo lee, frunce el ceño y dice: "No lo entiendo." Marisol sonríe. "Lo va a entender. Pruébelo una semana." Se va murmurando, pero se lleva el impreso.

La tarde está ajetreada con vacunas escolares y resfríos. Marisol limpia las salas de examen entre pacientes y no se sienta hasta las cuatro.

## Grammar

El verbo "clean" quiere decir "limpiar" en español. Es un verbo regular, así que el pasado es "cleaned". También repasamos "happen" y "hurt" de antes de esta semana.

Pattern Practice:

1. I ___ Camy's knee before I put a bandage on it. (limpié)
2. Something unexpected ___ with Mr. Reyes at the clinic. (pasó)
3. The patient's back ___ when he lifts boxes. (duele)

## Examples

1. Mr. Reyes comes in for sciatica and wants something strong.
2. Dra. Pérez tells him the clinic can only authorize over-the-counter medicine.
3. Marisol and Dra. Pérez talk about the patient.
Dra. Pérez: "Marisol, Mr. Reyes wants a prescription painkiller for his sciatica."
Marisol: "But the clinic can only authorize over-the-counter medicine, doctor."
Dra. Pérez: "I know. Eight hundred milligrams of ibuprofen, that is all we can do."
Marisol: "He is not going to be happy."
Dra. Pérez: "Tell him to try it for a week. If the pain is still bad, he will come back."
Marisol: "I will explain the dosage. Two tablets with food, three times a day."
4. Marisol cleans the exam rooms between patients and does not sit until four.

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. El paciente tiene ciática y quiere un analgésico fuerte, pero la clínica solo autoriza ibuprofeno sin receta.
2. La mamá lleva a la niña al apartamento de la vecina porque es su turno pa' llevarlas a la escuela.
3. El señor Reyes no se ve contento con la dosis de dos pastillas.
4. La impresora zumba y le entrega el impreso al paciente.
5. La tarde está ajetreada con vacunas escolares y resfríos.
6. Si el dolor sigue fuerte, el paciente debe volver a la clínica.

English → Spanish

1. The doctor tells the patient that the clinic can only authorize over-the-counter medicine.
2. Marisol cleans the exam rooms between patients and does not sit until four.

## Student Questions

1. Did you give over-the-counter medicine to your child this week? (sin receta)
2. What do you do when a client does not get it the first time you explain something? (entender)
3. Did you get better from a cold or a flu this week? (mejorarse)
4. Do you need a prescription to get strong pain medicine? (receta)
5. What do you do when you do not get it and need someone to explain again? (entender)

# FRIDAY

## Warmup

1. I ___ the bathroom before the patient comes in. (limpié)
2. A patient ___ his elbow yesterday. (lastimó)
3. I ___ to see my teacher at the mall. (pasó)

## Vocabulary

1. rolls out of bed – se levanta a rastras
2. usually – normalmente
3. a busy day – un día ajetreado
4. smashed finger – dedo aplastado
5. send to the ER – mandar a emergencias

## Story

**English:**

Marisol rolls out of bed already thinking about work. Friday is usually a busy day. It seems like Fridays are the day people find new ways to hurt themselves at their jobs. She arrives at the clinic, and there are already five cars in the parking lot.

Dra. Pérez is already texting her about patients before Marisol even parks the car. Mr. Ortiz comes in holding his hand. He smashed his finger fixing a fence at work. Marisol looks at it and winces. The finger is purple and swollen.

"That does not look good, Mr. Ortiz," Marisol says.

Dra. Pérez examines it, says he needs an X-ray, and sends him straight to the ER. Mr. Ortiz nods and goes.

Marisol cleans the exam room and writes the notes. It will be a busy day, probably twenty patients, and it is not even nine o'clock. She makes coffee and sits for two minutes before the next one walks in.

**Spanish:**

Marisol se levanta a rastras ya pensando en el trabajo. Viernes normalmente es un día ajetreado. Parece que los viernes son el día que la gente encuentra formas nuevas de lastimarse en el trabajo. Llega a la clínica y ya hay cinco carros en el estacionamiento.

La Dra. Pérez ya le está mandando mensajes sobre pacientes antes de que Marisol estacione el carro. El señor Ortiz entra sosteniendo su mano. Se aplastó un dedo arreglando una cerca en el trabajo. Marisol lo mira y hace una mueca. El dedo está morado e hinchado.

"Eso no se ve bien, señor Ortiz," dice Marisol.

La Dra. Pérez lo examina, dice que necesita una radiografía y lo manda directo a emergencias. El señor Ortiz asiente y se va.

Marisol limpia la sala de examen y escribe las notas. Va a ser un día ajetreado, probablemente veinte pacientes, y ni son las nueve. Se hace un café y se sienta dos minutos antes de que entre el siguiente.

## Grammar

Hoy practicamos el pasado de nuestros tres verbos: happened, hurt, and cleaned. "Happen" y "clean" son regulares, así que se les pone -ed. "Hurt" es irregular: hurt, hurt, hurt.

Pattern Practice:

1. I ___ the kitchen after work. (limpié)
2. The accident ___ at the parking lot. (pasó)
3. He ___ his finger fixing a fence. (lastimó)

## Examples

1. Marisol rolls out of bed already thinking about work.
2. Mr. Ortiz smashed his finger fixing a fence at work.
3. Marisol and Dra. Pérez talk about the patient.
Dra. Pérez: "Marisol, Mr. Ortiz's finger is bad. He needs an X-ray."
Marisol: "I saw it. It is purple and swollen. That does not look good."
Dra. Pérez: "Send him to the ER. I will write the referral."
Marisol: "He says he has to be back at work by two."
Dra. Pérez: "Well, the ER is faster than waiting here. He will understand."
Marisol: "I will tell him. It will be a busy day, probably twenty patients."
4. It will be a busy day, probably twenty patients, and it is not even nine.

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. El paciente se aplastó un dedo en el trabajo y la doctora lo manda a emergencias pa' una radiografía.
2. La mamá se levanta a rastras ya pensando en el trabajo.
3. El dedo del paciente está morado e hinchado.
4. La asistente limpia la sala de examen y escribe las notas.
5. Viernes normalmente es un día ajetreado en la clínica.
6. La doctora le dice al paciente que necesita una radiografía.

English → Spanish

1. The doctor examines the finger and says he needs an X-ray.
2. Marisol makes coffee and sits for two minutes before the next patient walks in.

## Student Questions

1. Do you usually roll out of bed early on work days? (normalmente)
2. Was today a busy day at your work? (día ajetreado)
3. Did you ever see someone with a smashed finger? (dedo aplastado)
4. What do you do when you need to send a child to the ER? (mandar a emergencias)
5. Do you take a short break for coffee on a busy day? (día ajetreado)

# SATURDAY

## Warmup

1. I ___ the trash out of my car today. (limpiaré)
2. A patient asks if the syringe ___. (dolerá)

## Vocabulary

1. school project – proyecto escolar
2. poster board – cartulina
3. checkbook – chequera
4. pay the bills – pagar las cuentas
5. a load of laundry – una lavada de ropa

## Story

**English:**

Saturday is Marisol's day off, but it does not feel like a full break. Camy needs help with her school project in the morning, and the poster board is still blank. Marisol sits with her at the kitchen table before the rest of the day gets away from her.

Marisa stops by. "You still want to walk in the park, right?" she asks. They agree to go once Camy's project is done. Marisol tells Camy to clean her room while she starts a load of laundry and opens the checkbook to pay the bills.

Her phone buzzes on the table. It is a text from Rod: "Hey, you free later?" Marisol reads it, smiles a little, and sets the phone face down. There will be time to answer him tonight. Maybe, if everything gets done, the four of them can still go to a movie. She looks at the blank poster board, the wet laundry, the open checkbook, and takes a breath.

**Spanish:**

Sábado es el día libre de Marisol, pero no se siente como un descanso completo. Camy necesita ayuda con su proyecto escolar en la mañana, y la cartulina todavía está en blanco. Marisol se sienta con ella en la mesa de la cocina antes de que el resto del día se le escape.

Marisa pasa por ahí. "¿Todavía quieres caminar en el parque, no?" pregunta. Acuerdan ir en cuanto Camy termine el proyecto. Marisol le dice a Camy que limpie su cuarto mientras ella pone una lavada de ropa y abre la chequera pa' pagar las cuentas.

Su teléfono vibra sobre la mesa. Es un mensaje de Rod: "Oye, ¿estás libre más tarde?" Marisol lo lee, sonríe un poquito y deja el teléfono boca abajo. Habrá tiempo pa' responderle esta noche. Quizás, si todo se hace, las cuatro pueden ir al cine. Mira la cartulina en blanco, la ropa mojada, la chequera abierta, y toma aire.

## Grammar

Hoy usamos el futuro con "will": will happen, will hurt, will clean. "Will" te dice que algo va a pasar más adelante. El verbo que va después de "will" se queda en su forma base.

Pattern Practice:

1. What ___ today? (pasará)
2. She ___ the kitchen after the project is done. (limpiará)

## Examples

1. Camy needs help with her school project, and the poster board is still blank.
2. Marisol is going to start a load of laundry and open the checkbook.
3. Marisol and Marisa talk at the kitchen table.
Marisa: "You still want to walk in the park, right?"
Marisol: "Yes, but first Camy needs to finish her project. The poster board is still blank."
Marisa: "Tell her to clean her room while you do the laundry."
Marisol: "I am going to open the checkbook too. I have to pay the bills."
Marisa: "Well, at least the four of you can still go to a movie tonight."
Marisol: "Maybe. If everything gets done."
4. There will be time to answer Rod tonight, maybe.

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

# SUNDAY

## Warmup

1. I ___ the kitchen yesterday. (limpié)

## Vocabulary

2. heal – sanar
3. over-the-counter – sin receta

## Story

**English:**

Sunday is quiet. Marisol lies in bed for a while, the sheets cool against her arms, no alarm, no busy day like Friday. Camy vuelve con tierra en las rodillas y pregunta si pueden tener arroz con leche pa' postre. Marisol dice que sí. La semana terminó. ## Grammar

Hoy mezclamos el pasado y el futuro: happened, hurt, cleaned, will happen, will hurt, will clean. El pasado es pa' hablar de lo que ya pasó. "Will" es pa' hablar de lo que va a pasar después.

Pattern Practice:

1. I ___ the wound before it heals. (limpiaré)
2. The knee ___ a little last night. (dolió)

## Examples

1. Sunday is quiet, and Marisol enjoys a morning with no tight schedule.
2. She video calls Ernesto, and Camy grabs the phone to say a quick hello to her tío.
3. Marisol and Ernesto talk on the video call.
Marisol: "Ernesto, I still need those papers for the business. Do you remember what I'm missing?"
