# MONDAY

## Vocabulary Review

1. vital signs – signos vitales
2. pulse – pulso
3. beats per minute – latidos por minuto
4. fever – fiebre

## Grammar Focus

Mr. Salazar has a fever. He is one person, so "have" changes to "has."

## Mini Story

1. Marisa drives Camy and Donna to school on Monday morning.
2. Camy tells her mom, "Don't be nervous. You know this job."
3. Letia reviews the five vital signs with Marisol at the clinic.
4. Mr. Salazar feels hot and tired, and he has a fever.
5. Marisol's hands shake a little, and she counts his pulse again.
6. His pulse is 96 beats per minute, and that is normal.

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. Letia repasa con Marisol los cinco signos vitales en la clínica.
2. El señor Salazar tiene fiebre, y la doctora lo va a ver.

English → Spanish

1. The normal range for an adult is 60 to 100.

## Student Questions

1. How many times does Marisol count Mr. Salazar's pulse? (contar)
2. Do you feel nervous when you start a new job? (nerviosa)

2. systolic / diastolic – sistólica / diastólica (el número de arriba / el número de abajo)
3. cuff size – tamaño del manguito
4. a little high – un poco alta

## Grammar Focus

Marisol gets a bigger cuff for Mrs. Gómez. Marisol is "she," so the verb is "gets."

1. Marisol gets the blood pressure cuff from the drawer for Mrs. Gómez.
2. The cuff is too small, and she gets a bigger cuff.

4. Mrs. Gómez says she always gets nervous at the doctor.

6. The second reading is lower, and Marisol writes both in the chart.

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. El manguito es muy chiquito, y Marisol saca uno más grande.
2. La señora Gómez siempre se pone nerviosa en el médico.

English → Spanish

1. Marisol writes both readings in the chart and tells Dr. Pérez.

## Student Questions

2. Do you get nervous at the doctor? (ponerse nerviosa)

## Grammar Focus

Marisol gives Ms. Ortega a polish remover pad. Marisol is "she," so the verb is "gives."

1. Marisol counts 16 breaths per minute and watches her chest.
