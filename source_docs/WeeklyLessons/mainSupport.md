# MONDAY

## Warmup

1. I ___ a little off this morning, so I drink extra coffee before work. (me siento)
2. Mrs. Carter ___ light-headed, so I tell Letia right away. (se siente)
3. I ___ tired last night, but I arrive at the clinic at 9:00. (me sentí)

## Vocabulary

1. a little off – no muy bien / un poco rara
2. let's get the ball rolling – vamos a empezar / manos a la obra
3. light-headed – mareado / mareada
4. warning sign – señal de alerta
5. hang tight / please wait – espere un momentico / favor de esperar (casual / formal)

## Story

**English:**

Monday is not Marisol's carpool day. Marisa drives the girls to school. Marisol feels a little off this morning. She did not sleep well, and the bus made her head heavy. She arrives at the clinic at 9:00. She walks in and says, "Good morning! Let's get the ball rolling." It is a phrase from Rosie, and it still makes her feel silly.

At 9:30, Mrs. Carter waits at the front desk. She tells Marisol, "I feel light-headed, and my heart beats fast." Marisol remembers from training that light-headedness and fast heartbeat can be warning signs. She stays calm. "Hang tight. Please sit down," she says.

Marisol takes her pulse: 112 beats per minute. That is above the normal range of 60 to 100. Her hands are steady, but her stomach turns. She tells Letia right away. She writes in the chart: "Light-headed, fast pulse, started this morning."

Dr. Pérez sees Mrs. Carter next. Marisol goes back to the front desk. The corner coffee machine smells strong. She takes a breath and says, "Okay, who's next?"

**Spanish:**

Lunes no es el día del rutero de Marisol. Marisa lleva a las niñas a la escuela. Marisol se siente un poco rara esta mañana. No durmió bien, y el bus le dejó la cabeza pesada. Llega a la clínica a las 9:00. Entra y dice: "¡Buenos días! ¡Manos a la obra!" Es una frase de Rosie, y todavía le da vergüenza decirla.

A las 9:30, la señora Carter está en la recepción. Le dice a Marisol: "Me siento mareada y me late el corazón rápido." Marisol recuerda de su capacitación que mareo y latido rápido pueden ser señales de alerta. Se mantiene tranquila. "Espere un momentico. Siéntese, por favor," dice.

Marisol le toma el pulso: 112 latidos por minuto. Eso está por encima del rango normal de 60 a 100. Sus manos están firmes, pero le revuelve el estómago. Le dice a Letia enseguida. Escribe en el expediente: "Mareada, pulso rápido, empezó esta mañana."

La Dra. Pérez atiende a la señora Carter después. Marisol vuelve a la recepción. La cafetera de la esquina huele fuerte. Toma aire y dice: "Bien, ¿quién sigue?"

## Grammar

"Feel" significa "sentirse" en español. En el presente, se dice "I feel," "you feel," "she feels" (con -s para él, ella, o ello). En el pasado, es irregular: "I felt," "she felt" — sin la terminación -ed.

Pattern Practice:

1. The patient ___ a little off this morning. (se siente)
2. You ___ fine after the check-up. (se siente)
3. She ___ light-headed at the clinic. (se siente)

## Examples

1. Marisol feels a little off this morning, so she drinks extra coffee before the first patient.
2. When a patient feels light-headed, Marisol tells Letia right away.
3. Camy feels tired after a long day at school, so she takes a nap before dinner.
4. Marisol: "You look a little off today. Did you sleep?"
Rosie: "Not well. The bus made my head heavy."
Marisol: "No worries. Let's get the ball rolling."
Rosie: "I feel better now that I have coffee."
Marisol: "That's what I like to hear."

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. La paciente se siente mareada y su pulso es de 112 latidos por minuto.
2. Marisol se siente un poco rara porque no durmió bien.
3. Un latido rápido del corazón puede ser una señal de alerta.
4. Favor de esperar un momentico, por favor.
5. Marisol le dice a Letia: "La señora Carter tiene el pulso rápido, 112 por minuto."
6. Marisol dice: "¡Buenos días! ¡Manos a la obra!"

English → Spanish

1. Marisol takes her pulse: 112 beats per minute.
2. The bus made her head heavy this morning.

## Student Questions

1. What does Marisol do first when Mrs. Carter feels light-headed at the front desk? (toma el pulso)
2. Why does Marisol tell Letia right away instead of waiting? (señal de alerta)
3. Do you ever feel a little off in the morning before you go to work? (un poco rara)
4. What does Marisol write in the chart about Mrs. Carter? (mareada, pulso rápido)

**Spanish:**

Le dice a Letia y escribe en el expediente: "Retortijones, débil, presión baja." Luego grita: "¿Quién sigue?"

## Grammar

"Tell" significa "decir" o "contar" en español. En el presente, "she tells" lleva -s, pero "you tell" no. En el pasado, es irregular: "I told," "she told" — sin la terminación -ed.

Mr. Delgado: "Thank you. I feel weak."
Marisol: "Please sit down. I will get you some water."

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. El paciente tiene retortijones y la presión arterial está baja, 98 sobre 60.

# THURSDAY

## Warmup

1. I ___ the manual cuff from the cabinet when the machine does not work. (traigo)
2. His wife ___ his medication list to the clinic. (trae)
3. I ___ the chart to Dr. Pérez after I write the numbers. (llevo)

## Vocabulary

1. manual cuff – manguito manual
2. stethoscope – estetoscopio
3. old-school – a la antigua
4. joint pain – dolor en las articulaciones
5. swelling – hinchazón
6. recent travel – viaje reciente

## Story

**English:**

Thursday morning, the blood pressure machine has a red light. The battery is dead. Marisol sighs. "Old-school today," she says. She brings the manual cuff and stethoscope from the cabinet. The stethoscope feels cold in her hand.

The first patient is Yoel Martínez, in his thirties. He walks slowly. His wife brings the medication list. Yoel tells Marisol about joint pain in his knees, ankles, and wrists. He missed three days. Marisol sees swelling in his wrists. His temperature is normal.

Marisol uses the manual cuff. A baby cries next door, so she waits for quiet. The first tap is the top. When the sound stops, that is the bottom. The reading is 124 over 78.

Marisol asks, "Did you travel recently?" Yoel says, "I came from Pinar del Río four months ago. Many people in my neighborhood had chikungunya."

Marisol writes in the chart and tells Dr. Pérez. She thinks about Jorge in Cuba, where the power goes out for hours and the flies are bad. She keeps working to help them.

Dr. Pérez says, "Good question about the travel."

**Spanish:**

El jueves por la mañana, la máquina de presión tiene una luz roja. La batería está descargada. Marisol suspira. "Hoy a la antigua," dice. Trae el manguito manual y el estetoscopio del gabinete. El estetoscopio está frío en su mano.

El primer paciente es Yoel Martínez, en sus treinta. Camina despacio. Su esposa trae la lista de medicamentos. Yoel le cuenta a Marisol del dolor en las articulaciones de sus rodillas, tobillos y muñecas. No pudo trabajar tres días. Marisol ve hinchazón en sus muñecas. Su temperatura es normal.

Marisol usa el manguito manual. Un bebé llora en la sala de al lado, así que espera a que se haga silencio. El primer golpe es el número de arriba. Cuando el sonido se detiene, ese es el de abajo. La lectura es 124 sobre 78.

Marisol pregunta: "¿Viajó recientemente?" Yoel dice: "Vine de Pinar del Río hace cuatro meses. Muchos en mi barrio tuvieron chikungunya."

Marisol escribe en el expediente y le dice a la Dra. Pérez. Piensa en Jorge en Cuba, donde la luz se va por horas y las moscas andan feas. Sigue trabajando para ayudar a su familia.

La Dra. Pérez dice: "Buena pregunta sobre el viaje."

## Grammar

"Bring" significa "traer" — venir hacia aquí, hacia quien habla. "Take" significa "llevar" — ir a otro lugar, alejándose de quien habla. En el pasado, ambos son irregulares: "I brought," "I took" — sin la terminación -ed.

Pattern Practice:

1. Marisol ___ the manual cuff from the cabinet. (trae)
2. I ___ my stethoscope to work every day. (traigo)
3. Can you ___ me the chart? (traer)

## Examples

1. When the blood pressure machine stops working, Marisol brings the manual cuff and stethoscope from the cabinet.
2. Yoel tells Marisol that his knees and wrists hurt when he walks.
3. Marisol feels the cold metal of the stethoscope and waits for the baby to stop crying.
4. Camy brings her homework to the kitchen table and tells her mom she needs help.
5. Marisol: "The battery is dead. Old-school today."
Rosie: "You bring the manual cuff, and I'll take the chart to Dr. Pérez."
Marisol: "Good. I feel better when I have the stethoscope in my hand."
Rosie: "You always feel that way on hard days."
Marisol: "Ha! Yeah, I do."

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. El paciente tiene dolor en las articulaciones de las rodillas, tobillos y muñecas, con hinchazón en las muñecas.
2. Marisol trae el manguito manual y el estetoscopio del gabinete.
3. Yoel le cuenta a Marisol que vino de Pinar del Río hace cuatro meses.
4. Marisol siente el estetoscopio frío en su mano.
5. La lectura de la presión es 124 sobre 78.
6. Marisol pregunta: "¿Viajó recientemente?"

English → Spanish

1. Marisol brings the manual cuff to the patient's room.
2. The patient's wife brings the medication list to the front desk.

## Student Questions

2. What does Yoel tell Marisol about his knees, ankles, and wrists? (dolor articular)

# FRIDAY

## Warmup

1. I ___ a small face mask for the boy when he has a fever. (traigo)
2. The boy ___ hot and tired, so I take him to a private room. (se siente)

## Vocabulary

1. rash – sarpullido
2. spreading – regándose / extendiéndose
3. face mask – nasobuco
4. private room – cuarto aparte
5. vaccine record – tarjeta de vacunas
6. we're going to take care of him – lo vamos a atender

## Story

**English:**

Friday afternoon, a mother rushes in with her little boy, Andrés. He is four years old. He has a fever and a light cough. A red rash starts on his face and is spreading down his neck.

Marisol knows the rule for fever with a rash. She brings a small face mask for Andrés and one for his mother. She takes them to a private room and closes the door. Some germs stay in the air and travel far, so the door stays closed.

The mother is scared. Marisol tells her in a calm voice, "We're going to take care of him." The mother's hands shake, and she clutches the phone in her pocket.

Marisol asks for his vaccine record. The mother finds a photo of the card on her phone. Marisol tells Dr. Pérez right away. Dr. Pérez examines Andrés and says, "We need to test him and call the health department."

Marisol washes her hands and feels proud. She did every step in order. The soap smells like lemons, and the water is warm.

**Spanish:**

Un viernes por la tarde, una mamá entra corriendo con su niño, Andrés. Tiene cuatro años. Tiene fiebre y una tos ligera. Un sarpullido rojo empieza en su cara y se está regando por el cuello.

Marisol conoce la regla de la clínica para fiebre con sarpullido. Trae un nasobuco chiquito para Andrés y otro para su mamá. Los lleva a un cuarto aparte y cierra la puerta. Algunos gérmenes se quedan en el aire y viajan lejos, así que la puerta se queda cerrada.

La mamá está asustada. Marisol le dice con voz tranquila: "Lo vamos a atender." Las manos de la mamá tiemblan y se aprieta el teléfono en el bolsillo.

Marisol le pide su tarjeta de vacunas. La mamá encuentra una foto de la tarjeta en su teléfono. Marisol le dice a la Dra. Pérez enseguida. La Dra. Pérez le revisa a Andrés y dice: "Hay que hacerle una prueba y llamar al departamento de salud."

Marisol se lava las manos y se siente orgullosa. Hizo cada paso en orden. El jabón huele a limón y el agua está tibia.

## Grammar

"Feel," "tell" y "bring" son todos irregulares en el pasado: "I felt," "I told," "I brought." En el presente, "she feels," "she tells," "she brings" llevan -s, pero "you feel," "you tell," "you bring" no. Recuerda: bring = traer (hacia aquí), take = llevar (para allá).

Pattern Practice:

1. A mother ___ her little boy to the clinic. (trae)
2. Marisol ___ proud after she washes her hands. (se siente)
3. I ___ the nurse the numbers when the reading is low. (le digo)

## Examples

1. Marisol brings two face masks to the front desk before the mother and boy walk in.
2. The mother tells Marisol that her son has a fever and a light cough.
3. Marisol feels proud of herself after she finishes every step in order.
4. Camy tells her mom that she feels scared of the dark at night.
5. Marisol: "We're going to take care of him. Please sit down."
Mother: "Thank you. I'm shaking. Can you bring water?"
Marisol: "No worries. I will tell Dr. Pérez right away."
Mother: "My hands won't stop. Can you bring water?"
Marisol: "Of course. I'll bring it now."

Se siente un poco culpable, pero lo deja ir. Siempre cubre por otros. En el museo, Camy y Donna se pierden en el laberinto de espejos y corren en la pista una y otra vez. Al final del día, se siente llena de paz.

## Translation Practice

Spanish → English

1. El jueves, la batería estaba descargada, así que Marisol trajo el estetoscopio.
2. Camy le trae a su mamá otra taza de café el domingo.

English → Spanish

1. Marisol remembers her week: Monday, light-headed; Tuesday, stomach cramps.
2. Marisol feels calm because she chose her daughter.

## Student Questions

1. What does Marisol remember from Monday and Tuesday at the clinic? (mareada, retortijones)
2. Do you feel calm at the end of a long week of work? (tranquila)
3. What does Camy bring to her mother on Sunday morning? (café)
