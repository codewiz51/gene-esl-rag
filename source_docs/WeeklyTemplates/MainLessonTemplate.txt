#BEGIN RULES: LANGUAGE_LEVEL
Target level: A2 → A2+ (light B1 vocabulary allowed).
Use simple present, simple past, going to, can, should.
Avoid present perfect, past perfect, conditionals.

Subordination is allowed at the A2+ ceiling:
  At most 2 subordinate clauses per long-day story.
  Allowed connectives: when, after, because, while.
  One "if" per long-day story is acceptable
   (simple first conditional,
  e.g. "If she is late, she takes the bus alone.").
Short days (Wed, Sat, Sun): 0 subordinate clauses.
The story is short enough to be flat.

Avoid long medical explanations.

AIM FOR:
  One sentence per long-day story where the
  character has an interior moment: she worries,
  remembers, feels something, or makes a small
  private decision.
  One sentence is enough. It does not need to
  be the last sentence.
#END RULES: LANGUAGE_LEVEL

# CHANGED: loosened prosody; expanded connectors; added aim-for
#BEGIN RULES: SENTENCE_CONTROL
Aim for 8–14 words per sentence.
No sentence should drop below 5 words.
A few sentences per story may reach 16 words
when a subordinate clause or a compound
structure is in use.

Prefer periods over commas.
At most 2 commas per sentence. Prefer 1.
One comma per sentence is the norm.
Two is acceptable for a compound sentence
(e.g. "She tells him to rest, and she checks
the door before she leaves.").

Allowed connectors:
  and, but, because, so, then, also,
  first, next, finally.
These are all A2. Use them to vary rhythm.

Subordination: see LANGUAGE_LEVEL block.
  2 per long day. 0 per short day.

One main event per story.
One character reaction per story.
"Reaction" means the character feels,
notices, or decides something.
Not just "the next thing happens."

One patient per main clinical interaction
per day (except Friday).
Supporting characters may appear:
coworkers, patients in the waiting room,
family, neighbors.
They are not the main patient.
They add texture.

AIM FOR:
  No two consecutive sentences should start
  with the same word.
  At least one sentence per long-day story
  should contain a character speaking directly
   (dialogue in quotes or internal thought
  in plain text, no italics — this is
  plain HTML).
  e.g. "You need to rest, Carlos," she says.
  or  She thinks: I should not let him go
        home alone.
#END RULES: SENTENCE_CONTROL

#BEGIN RULES: STORY_LENGTH
Long days (Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri):
 140–180 words, English side only.
 The range prevents both truncation and
 padding.
 Do not pad to hit the floor.
 A 142-word story is better than a 152-word
 story with two filler adverbs.

Short days (Wed, Sat, Sun):
 6–8 sentences, English + Spanish.
 9 is acceptable if the story needs it.
 5 is not acceptable.

Vocabulary on short days: 3–5 items.
Warm-up on short days: 1–2 questions.
Examples on short days: 3–4 items.
Student questions on short days: 3–4 items.

Translation Practice items:
 8–14 words per item.
 They do NOT need to obey the story
 sentence-length rule.
 A 16-word translation item is fine.
 Translation items are analytic.
 They are not story prose.

Spanish→English: 6 items on long days,
 2 items on short days.
English→Spanish: 2 items on all days.

Translation Practice MUST NOT reuse
vocabulary words unless they appear
naturally in the story.
#END RULES: STORY_LENGTH

#BEGIN RULES: READING_TRANSLATION
Translation Practice MUST include at least one READING-FOCUSED item per day.
Reading-focused items MUST:
   - use clinic-relevant vocabulary
   - use natural English phrasing (not literal Spanish structure)
   - include one short professional instruction or note
   - include one short symptom or assessment description
   - include one short lifestyle or treatment recommendation
Spanish→English items MUST include at least one professional reading item.
English→Spanish items MAY remain simple, but MUST include one clinic-relevant phrase.
#END RULES: READING_TRANSLATION

# CHANGED: expanded to all sentences; added before/after examples
#BEGIN RULES: TRAILING_ADVERB
Do NOT end ANY sentence with a bare
temporal adverb:
  now, today, right now, this morning,
  this afternoon, tonight,
  ahora, hoy, en este momento,
  en este instante.

This applies to every sentence in the story,
not just the last one.

If a sentence needs a time reference to make
sense, restructure it:
  BAD:  "Marisol thinks he has heat
         exhaustion now."
  GOOD: "Marisol looks at Jeff's face
         and thinks: heat exhaustion."
  BAD:  "Jeff is too agitated for the
         reading now."
  GOOD: "Jeff keeps moving.
         She cannot get the cuff on his arm."

If the time reference is essential to
meaning, keep it, but it must carry
content, not padding.
  OK: "Tomorrow she will take the 7
       o'clock shift."
#END RULES: TRAILING_ADVERB

#BEGIN RULES: SPANISH_VOICE
In the Spanish column prefer active voice. Avoid passive constructions like "es traído", "es examinado", "es llevado". Use "Traen a...", "Llevan a...", "Lo examinan..." instead.
#END RULES: SPANISH_VOICE

# NEW: quality targets layer
#BEGIN RULES: STORY_QUALITY
These are quality targets, not hard
constraints.
They do NOT override HTML_LAYOUT.
They do NOT add sections, styles, or
formatting.
They govern prose only.

VARIETY ACROSS DAYS:
 No two stories in a week should share
 the same emotional register.
 Plan the week:
  One day can be calm and domestic.
  One day can be tense.
  One day can be funny or wry.
  One day can be small and quiet.
 The storyboard should show this spread.
 If the storyboard is flat, the model
 should add register variation within
 the character's voice, not invent
 new plot.

WORLD EXTENDS BEYOND THE CLINIC:
 Marisol lives in a barrio.
 She has a daughter, a neighbor, errands,
 a life, a past as a university student.
 Clinic days are clinic days.
 Non-clinic days (Mon, Sat, Sun) MUST
 feel different in setting, rhythm, and
 register.
 A Saturday at the gym should not read
 like a Tuesday at the clinic with
 the patients removed.

HUMOR IS WELCOME WHEN NATURAL:
 A wry aside. A small observation.
 A line of dialogue that makes you
 smile.
 Humor MUST NOT be forced.
 A story that does not need humor
 should not get humor.
 A story that is tense should not
 get a joke.
 The model judges this per story.

THE STUDENT IS A SPEAKER, NOT AN OBSERVER:
 This is the most important quality
 rule in the file.
 The student is a Cuban woman, 28–36,
 a single mother, formerly university-track,
 now working as a house cleaner.
 She is not a passive reader of
 Marisol's life. She is in the room.
 She speaks. She thinks. She chooses.
 At least one task per day must put
 her in first person.
 (See SECTION_CONTENT, Warmup and
 Student Questions.)
#END RULES: STORY_QUALITY

#BEGIN RULES: HTML_LAYOUT
All HTML output MUST follow these layout rules:

1. ALL TEXT CONTENT MUST BE WRAPPED IN <p> </p> TAGS UNLESS IT IS A HEADER (<h1>-<h4>) OR A LIST (<ul>, <ol>).

2. USE ONLY STANDARD HTML TAGS: <html>, <head>, <meta>, <h1>-<h4>, <p>, <ul>, <ol>, <li>, <table>, <thead>, <tbody>, <tr>, <td>.

3. DO NOT ADD ANY <style> TAGS, CSS, INLINE STYLES, COLORS, BORDERS, BOXES, BACKGROUNDS, OR DECORATIVE FORMATTING.

4. Only the bilingual story may use a table.
   The bilingual story MUST use a two‑column structure:
    <table>
      <tr>
        <td>
          <p>English paragraph 1.</p>
          <p>English paragraph 2.</p>
        </td>
        <td>
          <p>Spanish paragraph 1.</p>
          <p>Spanish paragraph 2.</p>
        </td>
      </tr>
    </table>

5. DO NOT ADD ANY OTHER CLASSES OR IDS.

6. Vocabulary MUST appear as an ordered list (<ol>) of bilingual word or idiom pairs.
   Pattern:
   English word or idiom – Spanish palabra o modismo.
   Do NOT use full sentences.
   Do NOT use clauses.
   Do NOT use punctuation except the dash.
   Vocabulary list MUST contain 4–6 items total.

7. Warmup, Grammar, Examples, Questions, Closing MUST appear in single‑column layout.

8. Insert ONE blank line between the two <html> documents.

9. Use UTF‑8 encoding for all Spanish text.

10. Do NOT nest <html> blocks.

11. Immediately after the opening <html> tag, the model MUST include:
    <head><meta charset="UTF-8"></head>
#END RULES: HTML_LAYOUT
