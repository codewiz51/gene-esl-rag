#BEGIN RULES: LANGUAGE_LEVEL
Target level: A2 → A2+ (some B1 vocabulary allowed).
Use simple present, simple past, going to, can, should.
Avoid present perfect, past perfect, and complex
conditionals. Simple first conditional is fine
(e.g. "If she is late, she takes the bus alone.").

Avoid long medical explanations.

AIM FOR:
  One sentence per long-day story where the
  character has an interior moment: she worries,
  remembers, feels something, or makes a small
  private decision.
  One sentence is enough. It does not need to
  be the last sentence.
#END RULES: LANGUAGE_LEVEL

#BEGIN RULES: SENTENCE_CONTROL
Aim for 4–16 words per sentence, don't count exactly.

Allowed connectors:
  and, but, because, so, then, also,
  first, next, finally.
These are all A2. Use them to vary rhythm.
Prefer periods over commas. One comma is
fine for a natural compound sentence using
an allowed connector.

#END RULES: SENTENCE_CONTROL

#BEGIN RULES: SPANISH_ACCURACY
Spanish MUST preserve accents (UTF‑8).
Do NOT strip accents.
Do NOT convert to ASCII.
Spanish must be natural Cuban Spanish.
Avoid literal translations.
Use corrections dictionary when relevant.
Use Cuban register dictionary when relevant.
#END RULES: SPANISH_ACCURACY

#BEGIN RULES: STORY_LENGTH
Long days (Mon, Thu, Fri, Sat):
 140–180 words, English side only.
 The range prevents both truncation and
 padding.
 Do not pad to hit the floor.
 A 142-word story is better than a 152-word
 story with two filler adverbs.

Short days (Tue, Wed, Sun):
 6–8 sentences, English + Spanish.
 9 is acceptable if the story needs it.
 5 is not acceptable.

Vocabulary on short days: 3–5 items.
Warm-up on short days: 1–2 questions.
Examples on short days: 3–4 items.
Student questions on short days: 3–4 items.

Translation Practice items:
 8–14 words per item.
 They do NOT need to obey the story
 sentence-length rule.
 A 16-word translation item is fine.
 Translation items are analytic.
 They are not story prose.

Spanish→English: 6 items on long days,
 2 items on short days.
English→Spanish: 2 items on all days.

Translation Practice MUST NOT reuse
vocabulary words unless they appear
naturally in the story.
#END RULES: STORY_LENGTH

#BEGIN RULES: READING_TRANSLATION
Translation Practice MUST include at least one READING-FOCUSED item per day.
Reading-focused items MUST:
   - use clinic-relevant vocabulary
   - use natural English phrasing (not literal Spanish structure)
   - include one short professional instruction or note
   - include one short symptom or assessment description
   - include one short lifestyle or treatment recommendation
Spanish→English items MUST include at least one professional reading item.
English→Spanish items MAY remain simple, but MUST include one clinic-relevant phrase.
#END RULES: READING_TRANSLATION

#BEGIN RULES: TRAILING_ADVERB
Do NOT end ANY sentence with a bare
temporal adverb:
  now, today, right now, this morning,
  this afternoon, tonight,
  ahora, hoy, en este momento,
  en este instante.

This applies to every sentence in the story,
not just the last one.

If a sentence needs a time reference to make
sense, restructure it:
  BAD:  "Marisol thinks he has heat
         exhaustion now."
  GOOD: "Marisol looks at Jeff's face
         and thinks: heat exhaustion."
  BAD:  "Jeff is too agitated for the
         reading now."
  GOOD: "Jeff keeps moving.
         She cannot get the cuff on his arm."

If the time reference is essential to
meaning, keep it, but it must carry
content, not padding.
  OK: "Tomorrow she will take the 7
       o'clock shift."
#END RULES: TRAILING_ADVERB

#BEGIN RULES: SPANISH_VOICE
In the Spanish text prefer active voice. Avoid passive constructions like "es traído", "es examinado", "es llevado". Use "Traen a...", "Llevan a...", "Lo examinan..." instead.
#END RULES: SPANISH_VOICE

#BEGIN RULES: HINTS_AND_BLANKS
Every Warmup blank, every Grammar Pattern Practice blank, and
every Student Questions item MUST include exactly one hint.

Hint format: a single word or one short fixed phrase, in
parentheses, placed immediately after the blank or after the
question mark.
  Blank example:    I ___ (hint) carefully before I leave.
  Question example: What should you do when there is an air
                     quality alert? (stay inside)

Hints MUST be exactly one word, or one short fixed phrase
(e.g. "should + verbo"). Never a list of options. Never a
full sentence. Never a synonym pair.

Every blank in Warmup and Grammar Pattern Practice MUST have
exactly one correct answer. Do NOT write a blank that could be
correctly filled by more than one item from that day's
vocabulary list.
#END RULES: HINTS_AND_BLANKS

#BEGIN RULES: NARRATIVE_REWRITE
Rewrite all Story Requirements bullets
into a full narrative.
Do NOT copy bullets.
Do NOT output bullet lists.
Convert all items into natural sentences.

The rewrite is an expansion, not a
transcription.
  Add one character reaction not in
  the bullets.
  Add one sensory detail not in the
  bullets (a sound, a smell, a texture,
  a light, a temperature).
  Use only words already in play:
  the day's vocabulary block, the weekly
  verb focus, and general A2 vocabulary.
  Do NOT import vocabulary from other
  days.
  Do NOT invent new vocabulary for the
  Vocabulary list.
  The story prose may use common words.
  The Vocabulary section is the only
  place vocabulary is constrained.

Each long-day story MUST contain:
  • One main event. Not a list of things
    that happen — one thing the story is
    about.
  • One moment of character interiority.
  • One small conflict, choice, or surprise.
    Not a crisis. A missed turn. A wrong
    name. A patient who cancels. A kid who
    will not stop talking. A coffee that
    spills. Something small that makes
    the story a moment instead of a list.
  • One sentence where a character speaks
    directly.
  • One patient per main clinical
    interaction (except Friday).
    Supporting characters may appear —
    coworkers, patients in the waiting
    room, family, neighbors — but they
    are not the main patient. They add
    texture.

Short days (Tue, Wed, Sun):
  • One character voice moment.
  • Even a review day should read as
    Marisol living through the recap —
    a porch, a drive, a quiet pause —
    not a bulleted list of what happened.
#END RULES: NARRATIVE_REWRITE

#BEGIN RULES: STORY_QUALITY
These are quality targets, not hard
constraints.
They do NOT override MARKDOWN_LAYOUT.
They do NOT add sections, styles, or
formatting.
They govern prose only.

VARIETY ACROSS DAYS:
 No two stories in a week should share
 the same emotional register.
 Plan the week:
  One day can be calm and domestic.
  One day can be tense.
  One day can be funny or wry.
  One day can be small and quiet.
 The storyboard should show this spread.
 If the storyboard is flat, the model
 should add register variation within
 the character's voice, not invent
 new plot.

WORLD EXTENDS BEYOND THE CLINIC:
 Marisol lives in a barrio.
 She has a daughter, a neighbor, errands,
 a life, a past as a university student.
 The storyboard's setting for each day
 decides whether it is a clinic day.
 A day whose storyboard is NOT set at
 the clinic MUST feel different in
 setting, rhythm, and register from a
 clinic day.
 A non-clinic day at the gym should not
 read like a clinic day with the
 patients removed.

HUMOR IS WELCOME WHEN NATURAL:
 A wry aside. A small observation.
 A line of dialogue that makes you
 smile.
 Humor MUST NOT be forced.
 A story that does not need humor
 should not get humor.
 A story that is tense should not
 get a joke.
 The model judges this per story.

THE STUDENT IS A SPEAKER, NOT AN OBSERVER:
 The student is a Cuban woman, 28–36,
 a single mother, formerly university-track,
 now working as a house cleaner.
 She is not a passive reader of
 Marisol's life. She is in the room.
 She speaks. She thinks. She chooses.
#END RULES: STORY_QUALITY

#BEGIN RULES: MARKDOWN_LAYOUT
All output MUST follow these Markdown layout rules.
This is plain Markdown text, not HTML. Do NOT use any
HTML tags anywhere in the output.

1. Each day heading MUST be exactly one line, using a
   single "#", ALL CAPS, the day name only:
     # MONDAY
     # TUESDAY
     # WEDNESDAY
     # THURSDAY
     # FRIDAY
     # SATURDAY
     # SUNDAY
   Do NOT use "Monday", "Day 1", "Week XX – Monday", or
   any other form. Nothing else may appear on that line.

2. Section headings MUST use "##" and MUST be exactly one
   of the following, spelled and capitalized exactly as
   shown:
     ## Warmup
     ## Vocabulary
     ## Story
     ## Grammar
     ## Examples
     ## Translation Practice
     ## Student Questions
   Do NOT invent new section headings.
   Do NOT reorder, rename, merge, or split these.
   Do NOT use "###" or any heading level deeper than "##"
   anywhere in the document.

3. The Story section MUST use exactly this structure —
   no table, no other markup:

     ## Story

     **English:**

     English paragraph 1.

     English paragraph 2.

     **Spanish:**

     Spanish paragraph 1.

     Spanish paragraph 2.

   "**English:**" and "**Spanish:**" are the ONLY bold
   text permitted anywhere in the document. Do NOT bold
   anything else.

4. Vocabulary, Examples, Translation Practice, Student
   Questions, and Warmup MUST use numbered Markdown lists
   ("1.", "2.", "3.", ...). Do NOT use "-" or "*" bullets
   anywhere in the document.

5. Grammar's Pattern Practice items also use a numbered
   Markdown list, continuing directly after the grammar
   explanation paragraph — no separate heading for
   "Pattern Practice", just the label "Pattern Practice:"
   as a plain text line followed by the numbered list.

6. Vocabulary list line format:
     English word or idiom – Spanish palabra o modismo
   One pair per numbered line.
   Do NOT use punctuation other than the dash.
   
7. Translation Practice MUST use plain text labels, not
   headings, to separate its two parts. There MUST be a blank
   line between each label and the numbered list that follows
   it — without that blank line, the label and list merge into
   a single run-on paragraph when converted to Word, and the
   numbering is lost:
     Spanish → English

     1. ...
     2. ...

     English → Spanish

     1. ...
     2. ...

8. Do NOT use any Markdown formatting other than what is
   specified above: no italics, no tables, no blockquotes,
   no code fences, no horizontal rules, no links, no
   images, no nested lists.

9. Use UTF‑8 encoding for all Spanish text. Preserve
   accents. Do NOT strip or convert them to ASCII.

10. Do NOT wrap the output in a code fence. Do NOT start
    or end the response with three backticks. Output raw
    Markdown text only.

11. Do NOT include any commentary, preamble, explanation,
    or reasoning before or after the lesson content.

12. Separate each day's content from the next day's "#"
    heading with exactly one blank line. Do NOT add any
    other separator (no "---", no "***").
#END RULES: MARKDOWN_LAYOUT
