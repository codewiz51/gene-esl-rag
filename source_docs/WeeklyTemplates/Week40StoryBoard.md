WEEK 40 – STORY BOARD

WEEKLY VERB FOCUS (reference only — the parser does not pass this section to the model):
The verbs progress from A1 "use," to A2 "utilize," to B1/B2 "employ."
Use – I use a thermometer to check the patient's temperature. – Uso un termómetro para tomarle la temperatura al paciente. (usar)
Utilize – She utilizes a tongue depressor to look at the throat. – Ella utiliza un depresor de lengua para mirar la garganta. (utilizar)
Employ – The dermatologist employs cryotherapy to remove benign skin lesions. – La dermatóloga emplea la crioterapia para quitar lesiones benignas de la piel. (emplear)
(¿Cuándo añadimos una -s? Con he, she o it: él, ella o una cosa. Con "you" nunca: decimos "you use", no "you uses", aunque en español se diga "usted usa".)
(Ojo: "employ" también significa dar empleo: "The clinic employs a very good MA." Esta semana lo usamos sobre todo como emplear un método o un equipo.)
(Esta es una semana de refuerzo: repetimos mucho vocabulario de la Semana 39 porque hace falta más práctica.)

# MONDAY STORYBOARD

Examples:
I use a thermometer to take a patient's temperature. – Uso un termómetro para tomarle la temperatura al paciente.
Camy uses a computer twice a week at school. – Camy usa una computadora dos veces por semana en la escuela.
I used a blood pressure cuff during the exam. – Usé un manguito de presión durante el examen.
He used a pulse oximeter to check the patient's oxygen level and heart rate. – Usó un oxímetro de pulso para medir el nivel de oxígeno y el pulso del paciente.

# === VOCAB MONDAY ===

# === END VOCAB MONDAY ===

1 - Monday is Marisol's day to take Camy and Donna to school.
2 - In the car, Camy says, "Mom, we have computer class today. I am excited!"
3 - Marisol is glad to hear that Camy is learning to use technology. She thinks about her own job: the clinic uses technology every day.
4 - The clinic opens at 9:30. Marisol arrives at 9:00 to prepare the two exam rooms and stock the cabinets.
5 - She thinks about the five measurements she will use in her assessments: pulse, breathing, blood pressure, oxygen and temperature.
6 - The first patient is Michael Lopez, a walk-in in his twenties. He has the sniffles and complains of chest congestion.
7 - Marisol uses a thermometer to take his temperature: 99.9, a low-grade fever, just under 100.4. Then she attaches a pulse oximeter to his index finger. His pulse is 96 beats per minute, a little fast for a young man but still normal. His oxygen level is 95 percent, a little low but still normal.
8 - His blood pressure is 135 over 90, a little high for a young man.
9 - Marisol writes the numbers in the chart and tells Dr. Pérez. Dr. Pérez will see Mr. Lopez next.

1 - Tuesday at the clinic is steady, with no rush.
2 - Marisol utilizes the blood pressure cuff for Mr. Rodríguez, who has a bad headache.
3 - The cuff is too small for Mr. Rodríguez's arm. Marisol knows a wrong cuff size gives a wrong reading, so she gets a bigger cuff.
4 - The reading is 138 over 88. The top number is systolic, and the bottom number is diastolic. It is a little high.
5 - Mr. Rodríguez says he gets nervous at the doctor. Marisol tells him, "Take a deep breath. Sit quietly for a few minutes, and your blood pressure may come down." She leaves the room to check the schedule.
6 - After five minutes, Marisol comes back and takes the reading again. It is lower this time. She writes both readings in the chart and tells Dr. Pérez.
