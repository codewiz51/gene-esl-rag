# MONDAY STORYBOARD

Introduce "have" – "tener"
Examples:
I have a migraine. – Tengo migraña.
I have to drive the girls to school. – Tengo que llevar a las niñas a la escuela.
What symptoms do you have? – ¿Qué síntomas tiene?

# === VOCAB MONDAY ===

migraine – migraña
refill a prescription – renovar la receta
symptom – síntoma
waiting room – sala de espera

# === END VOCAB MONDAY ===

- Marisa has to take her car to the mechanic, so Marisol drives Camy and Donna to school.
- The girls talk and laugh the whole way. Marisol drops them off and hurries to the clinic.
- The waiting room is full.
- The first patient is Mrs. Ruiz, a woman in her forties. She has a migraine.
- Marisol asks about her symptoms.
- Mrs. Ruiz: "I have to go to work. I only need to refill my prescription."
- Marisol: "I understand. But Dra. Pérez has to see you first."
- Mrs. Ruiz sighs and sits down to wait.
- Marisol understands. For many women, no work means no pay.

# === VOCAB TUESDAY ===

# === END VOCAB TUESDAY ===

- Tuesday is quiet at the clinic.
- Mr. Vega, a man in his fifties, comes in for a follow-up. He had knee surgery three months ago.
- Mr. Vega: "Doctora, I can walk to the store without pain!"
- Marisol: "I am not the doctor, Mr. Vega. But I can tell her."
- Dra. Pérez says his recovery is good. He has one more follow-up in two weeks.
- Mr. Vega smiles. He is happy again.

- Camy is in bed. Marisol sits at the kitchen table with a glass of coconut water.
- She thinks about Mrs. Ruiz with the migraine and Mr. Vega, who can walk again.
- She writes sentences in her notebook and reads them softly: "I have a headache. I can take a pill. I have to rest."
- The words feel easier than they did on Monday.
- Camy calls from her bed, half asleep: "Mami, can I have a cookie?"
- Marisol: "You can have one. But you have to brush your teeth again."

# THURSDAY STORYBOARD

Introduce "get" – light reinforcement of "have" and "can"
Light past tense is fine where the story needs it (got off).
Examples:
He got off the bus at the wrong stop. – Se bajó de la guagua en la parada equivocada.
You will get better with rest. – Te vas a mejorar con reposo.
Can I get a prescription? – ¿Me puede dar una receta?
I get it. – Entiendo.

# === VOCAB THURSDAY ===

get better – mejorarse
get off the bus – bajarse de la guagua
over-the-counter – sin receta
get it – entender
prescription – receta

# === END VOCAB THURSDAY ===

- Thursday afternoon is busy at the clinic.
- A young man comes in with a sore ankle. He got off the bus at the wrong stop and tripped on the curb.
- Marisol gets ice for his ankle.
- Dra. Pérez looks at the ankle. It is a sprain. He will get better in two weeks with rest and ice.
- Patient: "Can I get a prescription for the pain?"
- Dra. Pérez: "You do not need a prescription. You can get medicine over the counter. It is cheaper."
- Marisol gets it. Many patients cannot pay for a prescription.
- As he leaves, Amanda says, "Next time, get off at the right stop!" He laughs.

# === END VOCAB FRIDAY ===

- Friday is slow at the clinic. Marisol sits at the front desk and thinks about the week.
- On Monday, Mrs. Ruiz had a migraine and needed a refill.
- On Tuesday, Mr. Vega could walk to the store without pain.
- On Thursday, a young man got off the bus at the wrong stop and hurt his ankle.
- Dra. Pérez stops by the desk.
- Dra. Pérez: "You look tired, Marisol."
- Marisol: "I had a long week. I had a lot of patients."
- Dra. Pérez: "I have good news. Crystal and Carla are going to work the weekends. You can have some time off."
- Marisol: "No more weekend shifts? I feel better already!"
- Marisol smiles at her desk. She can plan her Saturday with Camy.
