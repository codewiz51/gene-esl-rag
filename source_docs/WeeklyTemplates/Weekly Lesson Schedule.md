The weekly class and training schedule:
Monday: A low cognative load day.  The students have probably had a rough Sunday working.  Class is 45 minutes, followed by 30 minutes of discussion of words, Englsih, translating Cuban idioms to English.  I introduce a verb.
Tuesday: The students are away from work, we do not meet.  This is a day to perform housework, prepare meals for the week.  We do not meet on Tuesday,  The lesson is shorter and focuses on self study. A second verb is introduced. Story alternates between everyday conversation in the grocery store and taking care of matters, such as bills, insurance, internet service, etc.  Also focuses on children's school work.
Wednesday: The students are away from work, we do not meet.  This is a day to perform housework, prepare meals for the week.  We do not meet on Tuesday,  The lesson is shorter and focuses on self study. The verbs introduced on Monday and Tuesday are lightly reviewed. Story alternates between everyday conversation in the grocery store and taking care of matters, such as bills, insurance, internet service, etc.  Also focuses on children's school work.
Thursday: I start a heavier cognative load, focusing on clinic, formulating a patient instance that pushes a subject found in the Mometrix CMA study guide.  A third and final verb is introduced.  I introduce three verbs in a weekly lesson.
Friday: Significantly heavier cognative load.  Review the three verbs via examples and grammar instruction.  This is an in-person class.
Saturday:  Significantly heavier cognative load.  Review the three verbs via examples and grammar instruction.  This is an in-person class.
Sunday: A review of the week, written as a memory, the main character, Marisol reviewing the envents of the week in her mind.  In-person class.

All the in person classes follow the same time intervals as Monday.
I like to interleave personal, or non clinical storylines at time, to mimic real life.  
I want to change the student questions to have two multiple choice questions and one answer with a hint (usually a single Spanish word.)
