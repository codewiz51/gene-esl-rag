Marisol wakes up Monday at the usual time, the alarm already fading into the quiet of the kitchen. The house is still cool, the kind of early-morning hush that only exists before the day begins to stir. She fills the coffee pot and watches the dark liquid drip, the smell of fresh coffee filling the small room. She prepares a sandwich and a bag of chips for Camy, then adds an orange and a Hershey's Kisses candy on top of the plate, a small treat for the girl's lunch.

While Marisol is getting ready for work, she dreams of starting a business in her home. She pictures a small kitchen where she bakes, a desk by the window where she answers phones, a sign out front with her own name on it. When she came to this country, she dreamed of having a business of her own, something that was hers, not just a job with a clock and a supervisor. She wants to do something that she loves, something that makes her feel like more than a pair of hands on a schedule.

Being a medical assistant brings in money, but it is limited. There is a ceiling, and she can see it clearly now. She thinks, I must understand the requirements to start a business. She writes the thought on a napkin and folds it into her apron pocket, a small promise to herself.

Camy comes into the kitchen, backpack already on one shoulder, and says, "Mom, it's time to go. Donna is here." Marisol smiles to herself. Camy is her reality check.

The clinic is busy this morning, and the air conditioning hums hard against the Oklahoma heat. The waiting room is full, and the chairs are all taken. Many patients come in with heat-related illnesses and sunburn. Some are red and peeling on their shoulders; others are dizzy and pale, their lips cracked from the dry air.

Marisol uses the iPad to catch up on heat-related treatments, scrolling through protocols she has read a dozen times but never stops checking. Everyone is dehydrated and needs saline. Some need cool compresses to cool their foreheads, and the little ones cry when the cold cloth touches their skin.

During lunch, she calls her sister-in-law to ask if she can pick up the girls from school at 3:15. The phone call is quick, practical, the way these calls always are. Marisol must not plan during work hours. She knows this. The business dreams wait for the evening, for the quiet after Camy is in bed and the house is still.

# THURSDAY STORYBOARD

Introduce "must," light reinforcement of "dream" and "plan"
Examples:  
MUST (strong rule / obligation / necessity):
Marisol must focus on work. - Marisol debe concentrarse en el trabajo.
Marisol must wait to plan after work. - Marisol debe esperar a planear después del trabajo.
Dream (hopes, possibilities):
Amanda can dream that the phone won't ring. - Amanda puede soñar que el teléfono no va a sonar.
Letia dreams of a day off. - Letia sueña con un día libre.
Plan (to research a goal, steps to achieve something):
Parents should plan to check their children's vaccination records. - Los padres deben planear revisar los registros de vacunación de sus hijos.
Marisol must plan to learn how to start a business. - Marisol debe planear aprender cómo empezar un negocio.

# === END VOCAB THURSDAY ===

Marisol works the second shift at the clinic today. The afternoon light comes in low and golden through the windows, and the clinic is quieter than the morning. She calls her brother Ernesto and they decide to meet for coffee at his auto repair garage.

They sit in Ernesto's cluttered office, a small room at the back of the garage, with the smell of oil and small parts lying around on every surface. There are rags, wrenches, and a cold cup of coffee from yesterday. Ernesto pulls out a chair and gestures for her to sit.

Marisol asks outright, "What does it take to start a business in Oklahoma?"

Her brother smiles and says, "It is not hard to legally set up a business in Oklahoma. The hard part is deciding what you want to do." He leans back in his chair. "And you must be careful. Some jobs require certification by an educational institution. Some crafts require a license, with a test to be passed with a sufficient score. And some businesses do not have many requirements, but you need money for equipment."

He pauses, then says, "What I have learned is to decide what you want to do first. Write down ideas. Think about what talents you have. Make a plan. Tear it up and make another one. If it is something you can practice before starting the business, you should practice it first. If you love baking, you should make some of your favorite recipes and ask your friends to review your baking. If you want to cook, you should do the same thing. Make your food and have your friends and coworkers tell you if they like it."

Marisol smiles. Her brother has been helpful. It is time for her to go to work. She thanks her brother and hugs him, and she leaves for the clinic, the napkin from Monday still folded in her apron pocket.

Friday feels like a relief to everyone. There are clouds and a little rain, the kind of light rain that cools the pavement without soaking you. It is still warm, but it feels cooler than the last few days of 104-degree heat. Maybe the weather will get back to normal.

The girls are happy and giggling as Marisol drives them to school, their voices loud over the radio, singing a song she half-remembers from her own childhood. Marisol has a mischievous smile. After she drops the girls at school, she will stop at Starbucks for an expensive coffee, a small luxury she allows herself once a week.

She has been thinking of a small neighborhood coffee shop. Much simpler than Starbucks, with Cuban coffee, espresso, and a brewed decaf. She will bake her own pastries in the beginning, in her own oven, not an expensive commercial oven. Maybe this will be an idea for a small business. She dreams of it, just for a moment, the smell of coffee and warm bread filling a little corner shop, the sound of a doorbell when a customer walks in.

But for now: drop the girls at school, stop for a coffee to go, and scuttle off to work. As she arrives at the clinic, there are already two cars in the parking lot. Maybe Friday won't be too busy.
