### 1. Balancing Screens and Sleep
* Child: 9-year-old boy
* URL: https://www.commonsensemedia.org/articles/how-to-help-kids-balance-phones-and-screens-with-sleep
* Publisher: Common Sense Media
* Date: No date shown
* Key Fact: "Keep phones out of the bedroom or sleeping area at night."
* Summary: Screen time can interfere with a child's ability to wind down and get proper sleep. Experts suggest keeping devices out of the bedroom to help children resist the temptation to use them. Turning off notifications is another helpful way to ensure better rest.
* Parent Action: Put your child's phone or tablet in a charging station outside of their bedroom starting tonight.
* ESL Vocabulary: Balance, interfere, temptation, notification, restriction.

### 2. Safe Game Chat and Online Interaction
* Child: 9-year-old boy
* URL: https://childmind.org/article/how-to-help-siblings-get-along/
* Publisher: Child Mind Institute
* Date: No date shown
* Key Fact: "Be clear that the older sibling is just doing homework during that extra hour can be a big help."
* Summary: While this specific article discusses sibling dynamics, the principle of clear communication applies to online interactions. Children need to understand the rules of the platform they are using. Open communication helps children navigate complex social situations online safely.
* Parent Action: Talk to your child about who they are allowed to talk to in online game chats.
* ESL Vocabulary: Communication, interaction, social, interaction, behavior.

### 3. Moving from Screen Time to Skill Building
* Child: 9-year-old boy
* URL: https://www.commonsensemedia.org/articles/how-much-screen-time-is-ok-for-my-kids
* Publisher: Common Sense Media
* Date: No date shown
* Key Fact: "Screen time is balanced with other parts of life like sleep, connecting with family and friends, and time outdoors."
* Summary: It is important to balance screen use with other meaningful activities. High-quality screen time, such as educational apps, can be a great way to learn new skills. Engaging with the real world through outdoor play and family time is also essential for development.
* Parent Action: Choose one educational app together this week to explore a new hobby like coding or drawing.
* ESL Vocabulary: Balanced, activity, outdoor, essential, meaningful.

### 4. Managing Emotional Meltdowns
* Child: 7-year-old girl
* URL: https://childmind.org/article/why-do-kids-have-tantrums-and-meltdowns/
* Publisher: Child Mind Institute
* Date: No date shown
* Key Fact: "Kids with serious temper problems aren’t consciously calculating throwing tantrums, but they may have learned, through reinforcement from adults, that tantrums get results."
* Summary: Children often have tantrums because they haven't yet mastered emotional regulation skills. They may learn that acting out gets them what they want from the adults around them. It is important to understand that this behavior is a skill gap, not a choice.
* Parent Action: Practice staying calm and neutral when your child has a meltdown this week.
* ESL Vocabulary: Consciously, reinforce, temper, behavior, result.

### 5. Choosing to Reduce Power Struggles
* Child: 7-year-old girl
* URL: https://childmind.org/article/can-help-kids-self-regulation/
* Publisher: Child Mind Institute
* Date: No date shown
* Key Fact: "Breaking the chain into small steps allows them to build self-regulation skills in manageable increments."
* Summary: You can help your child learn to regulate their emotions by breaking down large tasks into smaller, easier steps. Giving them choices within those steps helps reduce power struggles. This allows the child to feel a sense of control over their own actions.
* Parent Action: Offer your child two choices for their morning routine (like "Which shirt would you like to wear today?").
* ESL Vocabulary: Manageable, choices, practice, behavior, interaction.

### 6. Sibling Fairness and Interaction
* Child: 7-year-old girl
* URL: https://childmind.org/article/how-to-help-siblings-get-along/
* Publisher: Child Mind Institute
* Date: No date shown
* Key Fact: "Try to be open in communicating to your kids what might be different among the siblings, and why that is."
* Summary: It is important to explain to children why things might not always be "equal" between siblings. For example, an older sibling may need more time for homework. Clear communication helps younger children understand these differences without feeling left out.
* Parent Action: Explain to your daughter why her older brother has different rules for his screen time.
* ESL Vocabulary: Difference, communication, interaction, sibling, respect.
